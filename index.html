Why this matters

More lessons. More hours. The same result.

Most players I meet practise more than the players who beat them. On the warm-up court the forehand is clean, the timing is there, and for a few games it even holds. Then the score gets close, the arm tightens, the feet go quiet, and the ball they've hit ten thousand times sails long.

So they do what they've always done. Train harder, book another lesson, copy another pro's technique off YouTube. For a week it feels different. Then the plateau is still there, and the next match goes the way they all go.

It isn't effort. It almost never is. It's that they're playing a game that was never built for them.

What I believe

Your body already knows how it wants to move.

For years you've been handed the same model. Hold the racquet like this. Follow through like that. Copy the players you watch on TV. But your body isn't their body. Forcing their technique is why your game feels mechanical, and why it falls apart the moment the pressure is real.

We are all different, so the work is to help you find your difference. Not to impose a technique, but to explore what your body does naturally and then build efficient, repeatable shots around it. When the movement fits you, it stops costing you anything to produce, and that's when it survives a tight scoreline.

Then you train the two things that actually decide matches: staying relaxed under pressure, and a plan clear enough to trust when the score is 4-4.

That's Ready2Perform. No forcing. No copying. Your most natural, most effective tennis.

Who I am

I got this wrong for years before I got it right.

I chased the tour from the age of six with a philosophy that was brutally simple: go hard, go hard again, push until you collapse. Exhaustion felt like proof I'd done enough. What it actually produced was over-training, constant injury, and a young man who was fit on paper and gassed on court.

Everything I got wrong became what I now teach. That's the coaching I do today, on the tour and online: individual, sustainable, and built on the mind as much as the body.
